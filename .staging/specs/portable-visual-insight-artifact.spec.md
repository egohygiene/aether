# Portable Visual Insight Artifact Specification

## Purpose

This specification defines a complete, portable system for transforming a scoped insight into a visually coherent, emotionally resonant, Pinterest-ready artifact.

The system combines:

- scoped insight extraction
- conceptual modeling
- visual metaphor selection
- emotional safety
- artistic compression
- Pinterest readability
- reflective design
- reusable publishing metadata

The final output should feel like:

> a discovered truth rendered as visual architecture.

It should not feel like:

- a corporate infographic
- a self-help worksheet
- a LinkedIn carousel
- a generic AI poster
- a dense educational chart
- a transcript summary
- a personal confession

---

# Core Philosophy

The goal is not to explain everything.

The goal is to reveal the shape of one meaningful idea.

The artifact should prioritize:

- understanding over coverage
- meaning density over information density
- symbolism over explanation
- emotional resonance over instructional tone
- visual intuition over textual completeness
- universality over autobiography

The central principle is:

> Do not merely explain the insight. Visualize it.

The viewer should understand the core idea intuitively before reading the supporting text.

---

# Generalization Rule

Source material may come from:

- therapy conversations
- journals
- relationships
- burnout
- identity shifts
- health experiences
- philosophical reflection
- career experiences
- spiritual exploration
- long-form conversations

However, the final artifact must not be tied to one person's biography.

Transform:

> personal experience

into:

> generalized human insight.

The viewer should think:

> “I recognize this pattern in my own life.”

Not:

> “This is clearly about one specific person.”

Preserve emotional truth while removing:

- identifying details
- specific people
- private chronology
- autobiographical dependence
- unnecessary oversharing

---

# Insight Scope

Each artifact must communicate one primary realization.

Not three.

Not ten.

One.

A strong insight should:

- stand on its own
- contain one coherent conceptual cluster
- remain understandable without source context
- generalize to a broad human audience
- preserve nuance without requiring exhaustive explanation
- translate naturally into one dominant metaphor

Avoid:

- mixed-topic artifacts
- giant conceptual summaries
- unrelated idea merging
- multiple competing theses
- oversized collections of bullet points

---

# Conceptual Pipeline

Use the following pipeline:

    Raw Reflection or Idea
        ↓
    Meaning Extraction
        ↓
    Conceptual Modeling
        ↓
    Understanding Compression
        ↓
    Dominant Visual Metaphor
        ↓
    Concept World
        ↓
    Artifact Generation
        ↓
    Pinterest Metadata

## Meaning Extraction

Identify:

- the central realization
- the emotional or philosophical core
- the recurring pattern
- the systems relationship
- the strongest tension
- the most useful reframe

## Conceptual Modeling

Determine whether the insight is best represented as:

- a loop
- a threshold
- a polarity
- a transition
- a collapse
- a reconstruction
- a feedback system
- a containment problem
- a developmental path
- an integration process
- an internal architecture

## Understanding Compression

Reduce the insight to:

- one title
- one core line
- one metaphor
- one visual world
- one optional short explanation

If extensive explanation is still required, the insight has not been compressed enough.

---

# Artifact Classes

## Type A — Insight Artifact

A single realization rendered symbolically.

Examples:

- Stability Feels Dangerous
- The Trauma Lens
- Intensity Is a Raw Material
- Compassion Without Self-Erasure

## Type B — Systems Artifact

A conceptual system or framework.

Examples:

- Gut–Brain Command Loop
- Recursive Self-Debugging
- Consciousness as Infrastructure
- Visual Synapse Pipeline

## Type C — Journey Artifact

A transition, transformation, or integration process.

Examples:

- Fragmented Identity Reconstruction
- Projection to Channeling
- Burnout as Initiation
- Memory Reclaimed Through Integration

Choose the artifact class that best fits the insight.

Do not force every concept into the same composition.

---

# Required Artifact Structure

## Layer 1 — Insight Number

For numbered series posts, place the insight number clearly but unobtrusively near the top.

Format:

    INSIGHT 099

Do not include hashtags or metadata inside the artwork.

## Layer 2 — Title

Use one concise, memorable title.

The title should feel:

- timeless
- conceptual
- human
- emotionally precise
- easy to read on mobile

Avoid:

- clickbait
- questions
- excessive specificity
- diagnostic language
- long subtitles

## Layer 3 — Core Line

Use one compressed sentence.

Maximum two short sentences.

This is the emotional center of the artifact.

Examples:

> The future is not the catastrophe.  
> The nervous system is forecasting through fear.

> Unprocessed pain projects.  
> Processed pain channels.

> Healing is not the absence of motion.  
> Healing is coherent motion.

## Layer 4 — Dominant Visual Metaphor

Every artifact must contain one primary metaphor.

Only one dominant metaphor.

Possible metaphors:

- mountain
- river
- bridge
- crystal
- spiral
- orbit
- tree
- network
- lighthouse
- storm
- path
- constellation
- forge
- mirror
- armor
- horizon
- threshold
- roots
- architecture
- reactor
- garden
- wave

The metaphor must communicate the insight without requiring text.

## Layer 5 — Concept World

Do not create a diagram surrounded by boxes.

Create a world in which the insight lives.

Ask:

- What landscape embodies this realization?
- What forces are interacting?
- What is transforming?
- What is becoming stable?
- What is dissolving?
- What is being rebuilt?
- What movement carries the meaning?

Examples:

### Stability

- calm lake
- distant storm dissolving
- nervous-system alarm reflected in still water

### Integration

- fractured crystal reforming around light
- spiral path returning to the same place at a deeper level

### Projection

- dark energy passing outward
- the same energy transformed into music, light, or structure

### Burnout

- burned architecture shedding excess weight
- a new structure emerging from the cleared foundation

## Layer 6 — Minimal Explanation

Optional.

Maximum one short paragraph.

Prefer:

- 20–50 words
- large readable typography
- one reframe
- one emotional implication

Avoid:

- long lists
- tiny labels
- repeated explanations
- dense educational sections
- “signs you may be experiencing this”
- generic advice panels

---

# Three-Second Rule

Someone scrolling Pinterest should understand the core idea within three seconds.

This requires:

- a readable title
- one dominant image
- obvious visual contrast
- one intuitive symbolic relationship
- minimal competing information

If the concept cannot be understood quickly, simplify the visual metaphor.

---

# Thirty-Second Rule

Someone who pauses should discover additional meaning within thirty seconds.

Depth may emerge through:

- visual layering
- symbolic contrast
- spatial relationships
- subtle secondary motifs
- transformation across the composition
- the relationship between title, core line, and image

The artifact should reward attention without demanding study.

---

# Artistic Compression

The deeper the insight, the simpler the artifact.

Use:

- one core idea
- one metaphor
- one emotional center
- one visual world

Avoid:

- multiple diagrams
- giant lists
- excessive concept labels
- encyclopedia-style posters
- repeated framing
- visual clutter

The image should carry most of the meaning.

Text should support the image, not compensate for it.

---

# Emotional Safety

The artifact should feel:

- compassionate
- grounded
- reflective
- emotionally intelligent
- non-accusatory
- non-shaming
- psychologically safe

Avoid:

- diagnosing the viewer
- presenting AI as clinical authority
- sensationalizing trauma
- glamorizing dysregulation
- portraying suffering as moral superiority
- declaring symbolic experiences to be literal facts
- treating metaphors as medical claims
- reducing complex behavior to good versus evil

When discussing trauma, nervous systems, consciousness, substances, or therapy:

- use careful, non-diagnostic language
- distinguish metaphor from biology
- avoid certainty where the underlying claim is interpretive
- center integration, grounding, and human support
- preserve accountability without dehumanization

---

# Human-First Tone

The artifact should feel:

- personal
- universal
- timeless
- intimate without oversharing
- intelligent without being cold
- philosophical without becoming vague

The ideal feeling is:

# Typography

Use:

- one elegant display serif or editorial title style
- one highly readable supporting typeface
- large title text
- generous line spacing
- strong contrast
- minimal word count

Whitespace is valuable.

Silence is valuable.

The artifact should feel confident enough to leave space.

Do not shrink text to fit more content.

Remove content instead.

---

# Pinterest Composition

Use a vertical Pinterest-friendly format.

Preferred behavior:

- strong title near the top
- dominant visual occupying roughly 45–70% of the composition
- minimal supporting text
- readable at phone size
- visual hierarchy clear at thumbnail scale
- no hashtags inside the image
- no unnecessary metadata inside the image

A practical target:

    Insight Number
    Title
    Core Line
    Dominant Visual World
    Optional Short Explanation or Reframe

The artifact should remain beautiful even if the viewer never reads the smallest text.

---

# Output Workflow

For each supplied insight:

1. Identify the one core realization.
2. Generalize it for a broad human audience.
3. Remove autobiographical dependencies.
4. Select the artifact class.
5. Select one dominant metaphor.
6. Build one visual world.
7. Compress the text.
8. Generate the Pinterest-ready image.
9. Provide Pinterest metadata separately.

Do not ask the user to choose among many design options unless the insight is genuinely ambiguous.

Use judgment and generate directly.

---

# Pinterest Metadata Output

After image generation, provide exactly two reusable text blocks.

## Block 1 — Pinterest Title

A concise, searchable title.

Usually match or lightly adapt the artifact title.

## Block 2 — Pinterest Description

Include:

- one short opening statement
- a brief explanation of the insight
- a generalized and emotionally resonant takeaway
- relevant hashtags at the bottom
- each hashtag on its own line

Do not include a separate hashtag block.

Do not include the insight number in the Pinterest title unless useful for catalog organization.

Keep the description broadly relatable and non-autobiographical.

# Quality Checklist

Before finalizing, verify:

## Concept

- Is there only one core insight?
- Is the insight generalized?
- Is the central reframe clear?
- Is the artifact understandable without source context?

## Visual

- Is there one dominant metaphor?
- Does the metaphor teach before the text?
- Does the composition feel like a world rather than a chart?
- Is the image readable at Pinterest scale?
- Is there enough whitespace?
- Is the style fitting rather than repetitive?

## Text

- Is the title concise?
- Is the core line emotionally precise?
- Is the explanation truly necessary?
- Can any text be removed?
- Are there no hashtags inside the image?

## Emotional Safety

- Does the artifact avoid diagnosis?
- Does it avoid glorifying suffering?
- Does it preserve accountability?
- Does it avoid autobiographical exposure?
- Does it invite reflection rather than impose interpretation?

## Final Test

The artifact succeeds if:

1. It attracts attention visually.
2. The core idea is understood within three seconds.
3. Additional meaning emerges within thirty seconds.
4. It feels timeless.
5. It feels uniquely human.
6. It feels like a discovered truth, not a lesson.
7. It can be related to without knowing the creator's story.
